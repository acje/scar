# 18 — Randomized replicated experimental design

Additional idea 4 of 5. Status: external candidate mechanism, not adopted SCAR policy.

## Mechanism

Randomize assignment or run order and replicate treatment levels when comparing
outcomes. The cited NIST guidance treats one primary factor and balanced replications;
randomization helps address order/allocation effects, while replication exposes
variation and supports sensitivity of comparisons under the applicable assumptions.

## SCAR relationship

SCAR requires evidence and the proposals ask for effectiveness assessment, but no
named randomized allocation/replicated comparative design was observed in surveyed
protocol, template, catalogue, proposals or eight skills. This is scoped absence,
not denial of all experimentation in history or automatic applicability to every task.

## Example

Compare two prompts on matched tasks using randomized run order and repeated runs.
Record model/context/hardware, costs, outcome definitions and nuisance factors.
Judge improvement separately from preservation of required obligations.

## Tradeoffs and limits

Replication costs budget; randomization does not eliminate all confounding or
measurement bias. Shared state, learning, task differences and evaluator reuse can
still undermine inference. Specify the task design before choosing sample counts;
no magic number, universal causal proof or significance threshold is imported.
SCAR protected obligations remain individual constraints, not statistical averages.
